What this is

This document says what every actuator on a DVT unit should do in each operating state, how the unit moves from one state to the next, and which rules hold in all of them. Section 8 then lists every place the current firmware does something different.

The spec has two sources. Alex wrote Caustic drain, Chlorine generation and Generation shutdown on 10/14. A three-round review on 10/19 settled the rest. Section 7 records each decision, and every cell in the table below cites the spec or a decision. Where the firmware or an older record disagrees, this spec wins and the difference goes in section 8.

The firmware check is a source read of standardwater/mainboard. Nothing was compiled or run. Two builds were read:

Build Role Commit Note
v1.10.119 Field candidate 2c2f354, 9/10, on main
v1.10.132 Newest, run by aging plan V3.x dbea6b8, on feature/chemistry-sensor-temperature Committed 10/13 and not merged to main. The Confluence OTA bin is dated 9/18 and the CHANGELOG entry 9/21, so nobody has yet shown this commit is the bin HSW tested. See HCG-342.

The behaviour this document cares about is the same in both builds except where section 8 says otherwise. v1.10.132 changed the start sequence and the scope of the flow and pressure faults. Nothing else in the state logic moved.

The table assumes four ball valves, because that's what the firmware drives and the CAD shows. Standing decision 7 and HCG-324 say the shipped batch went from three gas-line valves to one. If the shipped set differs, the B and C rows change.

The inputs used here are the leak sensor on P05, flow meter A (FL_A) and the pressure sensor at the venturi inlet, and four level sensors on P14 to P17. P14 is the lowest and P17 the highest. The sensors sit in the caustic chamber and read the level it shares with the brine chamber, so this spec calls them brine level sensors by function. The IO doc names P10 to P13 for the brine (salt) tank sensors and P14 to P17 for the alkali tank. The salt-tank sensors were never fitted, and P10 to P13 became relay outputs in v1.9.94. (Corrected 2026-10-21. The 10/19 text said the caustic tank had no level sensing and called P10 to P13 its inputs.)

The records disagree on where A and B sit. The firmware and the 8/6 CAD review put A on the water inlet. The PCB schematic V2 labels J26 "Brine Tank Inlet", and ARCHITECTURE.md §4.1 draws A between the brine tank and the cell. Alex placed B at the mixing column outlet on 10/19. ARCHITECTURE.md §4.1 and the 10/14 handoff put it between the gas-liquid separator and the column. This spec follows the firmware for A and Alex for B. Photographing the valves, NOTES Dashboard item 13, settles both.

Notes on the table:

  • Cells off includes the SSRs. Every "Off" for the electrodes means PWM at zero, cell enable low and both SSRs open.
  • The electrical fault column clears gas only if the fault arrives while gas is in the lines, from Chlorine generation or Generation shutdown. From any other state it goes straight to everything closed and off. That split is derived from D12, not decided outright.
  • P17 handling is the brine-high hold in section 3, row T10. The pinch closes for at most 15 s, then A closes and the pinch is released. It never stays powered closed long enough to hit the blockage Urim reported, open item O1.
  • Restart lockout is a flag on Standby, not a state. The outputs are the same as Standby. Section 6 covers it.

4. Interlocks

These hold in every state. They restate I1 to I6 from the 8/8 field note for DVT valve names, and add the ones the 10/19 decisions need. The field note's V_MC, a column outlet valve, is valve B on DVT.

# Rule Prevents From
I1 Cells enabled or SSRs closed only if A, B and C are open, D is closed, the booster is running, the FL_A and pressure check has passed after T_settle, no fault is latched and P17 is dry Cl₂ generated into a dead-ended or still line 8/8 I1, D6
I2 A open only while B is open. Outside T1 steps 2 and 3, A open also needs the booster running RO reject filling the column and backing up through the separator into the brine tank 8/8 I2
I3 D open only if A, B and C are closed, the cells are off with the SSRs open, and the booster has run for at least T_settle Caustic drawn alongside Cl₂, or caustic left in a still T 8/8 I3, I4
I4 Booster off only with A and D closed and the cells off with the SSRs open Inlet water or caustic with no motive flow to carry it 8/8 I4
I5 D opens only after a full Generation shutdown since the cells were last on, or from Standby Remnant Cl₂ in the column meeting caustic 8/8 I5, D3
I6 The pinch is powered closed only while A is open and P17 is wet, and for no longer than T_p17 at a time The blockage Urim sees with the pinch held closed, and powering a valve that does nothing with A shut D7, D17

The 8/8 note's I6, a hardware check valve downstream of the gas-liquid separator, isn't a firmware rule.

5. Timers and settings

D8 and D19 make the drain timers and the fault timer settable over MQTT without a reflash. This spec extends that to every sequence timer, using the pattern Urim already uses for setFlowALowThreshold: an MQTT command, a range check, and storage in NVS that survives a reboot. Alex to confirm the extension in review.

The separate drain-rate estimate puts the time to uncover the D port at 1 to 3 minutes at a typical site. That makes 10 minutes safe, but probably 3 to 10 times longer than needed. See Caustic drain rate estimate 2026-10-19.md.

7. Decisions record

S. 2026-10-14. Alex's spec for Caustic drain, Chlorine generation and Generation shutdown, quoted in handoffs/2026-10-14-hcg50-operating-states-table.md.

  1. 2026-10-19. Valve B is open only in Chlorine generation and Generation shutdown, to clear remnant Cl₂, and closed otherwise so the venturi pulls hard on D. D10 opens it again for the flush.
  2. 2026-10-19. The booster runs during Generation shutdown and in every state that sends material back to the pool. Without it the venturi has no suction.
  3. 2026-10-19. Generation shutdown is a fixed 180 s. Its only job is clearing remnant Cl₂. The dead "solenoid close time" screen setting goes.
  4. 2026-10-19. Standby has A, B, C and D closed, the booster off, the cells off and the SSRs open. C can close because nothing makes H₂ with the cells off, and Generation shutdown already cleared the lines.
  5. 2026-10-19. The caustic drains at the end of each run, and every T_int, about 2 h, during long runs. P14 to P17 are brine sensors, so the caustic tank has no level sensing. (Second sentence corrected 2026-10-21: P14 to P17 sit in the caustic chamber and read the shared level, so DVT1 has no caustic level separate from the brine. The timer drain stands as Alex's choice. HSW's 2026-10-16 Plan B would end a drain when level sensors B and C drop out.)
  6. 2026-10-19. Start order: valves, then the booster, then about 20 s for the hydraulics to reach steady state, then the flow and pressure check, then the SSRs, then the cells.
  7. 2026-10-19. The pinch stays open and closes when brine reaches P17. Valve A gates makeup, since it controls RO permeate and reject together. Urim reports that a pinch closed for more than about 20 s blocks the rest of the hardware. That needs troubleshooting, O1.
  8. 2026-10-19. T_int and T_drain are settable without a reflash. T_drain starts at 10 min. The drain-rate calculation became its own task, now Caustic drain rate estimate 2026-10-19.md.
  9. 2026-10-19. A user stop runs Generation shutdown and goes straight to Standby without draining. A stored drain-owed flag makes the next start run the drain first.
  10. 2026-10-19. Caustic drain shutdown flushes the venturi with RO reject through the mixing column. D closes, A and B open, the booster runs for about 60 s, then everything shuts down.
  11. 2026-10-19. B sits between the mixing column outlet and the venturi. With B and C closed all suction is on the caustic tank. If the caustic drops below the outlet, the venturi pulls air and sends bubbles to the pool, which isn't a critical failure. No hardware change.
  12. 2026-10-19. Hydraulic faults turn off the cells and open the SSRs first, then close the valves and stop the booster. Electrical and brine-level faults open the SSRs and close A, and the booster keeps running to clear gas. Without RO reject flowing that clearing is only partial.
  13. 2026-10-19. All four ball valves fail closed on a capacitor. A restart comes up in Standby, publishes an improper-shutdown alert, and sets drain owed. DMT needs a notification for the alert.
  14. 2026-10-19. At P17, the pinch closes. Cutting generation while the booster keeps running is the safer choice, and closing A is the escalation. D17 sets the details.
  15. 2026-10-19. The cell SSRs use the pins v1.9.96 set up. The code drives P10 and P11 in v1.9.96, v1.10.119 and v1.10.132.
  16. 2026-10-19. Schedule end, TimerOn expiry and serverStandby drain at the end of the run. Screen OFF, mode changes and SetControlMode to standby don't, and set drain owed. A server comms timeout isn't a stop, because the unit has to keep working offline.
  17. 2026-10-19. The brine-high hold runs as in T10, with T_p17 at 15 s and T_hold at 120 s.
  18. 2026-10-19. Power lost while in Standby: resume as normal. Power lost during generation or a drain sequence: the unit has to be checked before it runs again. Checking and clearing the flags works over MQTT as well as on site.
  19. 2026-10-19. The fault gas clear runs 180 s, settable over MQTT so it can be tuned without a new build.
